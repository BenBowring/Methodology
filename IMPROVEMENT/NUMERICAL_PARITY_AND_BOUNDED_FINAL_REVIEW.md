# Numerical Parity Recovery and Bounded Final Review

## 1. Position in the harness

**INTAKE → MVP / BLIND REPLICATION → PARITY RECOVERY → FINAL**

The blind replication is completed and frozen before parity recovery begins.

The stages answer different questions:

> **Blind replication:** Can the result be reproduced from the paper, supplied data, and the blind evidence boundary?

> **Parity recovery:** If not, can we determine what the authors actually did well enough to reconstruct the published result?

Parity recovery must never overwrite or retroactively improve the blind result.

---

# 2. Numerical Parity Recovery

## Purpose

The purpose of parity recovery is to distinguish:

1. a genuine scientific failure to reproduce;
2. an underspecified method whose hidden choices can be recovered;
3. missing or incomplete input data;
4. an implementation error in the replication;
5. a result that remains genuinely non-identifiable.

This stage is explicitly **target-aware**. The published numerical result may be used as evidence about the authors' hidden pipeline.

That is different from the blind stage, where choosing a method because it reproduces the target is forbidden.

---

## Entry conditions

A claim enters parity recovery when it is:

- `FAIL` under blind replication; or
- `NOT-REPRODUCIBLE` because of an identifiable data or methodology gap.

Blind `PASS` results normally do not enter parity recovery unless later evidence shows that their implementation is structurally suspect.

Before recovery begins, freeze:

- blind numerical value;
- blind score;
- blind code;
- preprocessing;
- parameters;
- evidence used;
- divergence diagnosis.

These remain permanently visible.

---

## Recovery workflow

### 1. Diagnose the miss

Classify the failure as one or more of:

- missing input data;
- hidden preprocessing;
- ambiguous convention;
- missing calibration;
- missing model or external dependency;
- underspecified algorithm;
- implementation defect;
- likely substantive disagreement.

The first objective is to explain the *shape* of the numerical discrepancy.

---

### 2. Generate recovery hypotheses

Use the direction and magnitude of the miss to propose methodological explanations.

Examples:

- factor-of-two discrepancy → radius versus diameter;
- timing mismatch → different event threshold;
- missing extreme value → smoothing or segmentation;
- photometric mismatch → zero-point, filtering, or survey alignment.

A hypothesis generated from the target is **not itself evidence**.

---

### 3. Expand the evidence boundary

Parity recovery may consult sources intentionally excluded from blind reconstruction, including:

- released author code;
- supplements;
- cited methodology papers;
- survey/instrument documentation;
- public calibration products;
- public raw/intermediate data;
- figure source data;
- repository configuration/history where relevant.

Every new source consulted is logged.

---

### 4. Allow target-aware experimentation

The agent may test methodological choices because they reproduce the published quantity.

For example:

> Does defining the eclipse at a 20% depth reproduce the published start, end, and duration?

This is allowed.

What remains disallowed is unconstrained optimisation of arbitrary knobs solely to minimise numerical error.

The objective is to reconstruct a plausible pipeline, not fit the answer.

---

### 5. Prefer joint recovery

A hidden choice is much stronger when the **same choice explains multiple outputs**.

Examples:

- one eclipse threshold reproduces start, end, and duration;
- one segmentation rule reproduces gradient distribution, maximum velocity, and plotted turning points;
- one calibration choice reproduces several bands or epochs.

A choice that fixes only one number remains weakly supported.

---

### 6. Seek independent confirmation

Target-inferred choices should, where possible, be confirmed by:

- author code;
- explicit Methods text;
- supplementary documentation;
- cited implementation;
- figure geometry/source data;
- multiple independent outputs generated by the same choice.

Numerical closeness alone is insufficient for a confirmed recovery.

---

### 7. Re-run downstream quantities

Once a recovered methodological choice is accepted, propagate it through the relevant pipeline.

Do not patch only the headline result.

The recovered pipeline should generate all dependent quantities naturally.

---

## Recovery status labels

Suggested states:

- `TEXT-SUPPORTED`
- `CODE-CONFIRMED`
- `DATA-CONFIRMED`
- `MULTI-OUTPUT-INFERRED`
- `TARGET-INFERRED`
- `NON-IDENTIFIABLE`
- `UNRECOVERABLE`
- `SUBSTANTIVE-DIVERGENCE`

The exact vocabulary can be simplified later.

The essential distinction is between **confirmed methodological recovery** and **target-fitting**.

---

## Permanent dual reporting

Blind and recovered results are always reported separately.

Example:

### Maximum transverse velocity

**Blind**

`0.67 km/s` — `FAIL`

Method: multi-night fitted segments under the blind-stage selection rule.

**Parity recovery**

`7.92 km/s` — parity with paper `7.5 km/s`

Recovered choices:

- diameter rather than radius convention;
- fine / near-adjacent turning-point segmentation.

Recovery status:

`MULTI-OUTPUT-INFERRED`, unless subsequently confirmed by author code or another independent source.

The recovered value never replaces the blind value.

---

## Stop conditions

Parity recovery ends for a claim when one of the following is reached:

1. **RECOVERED PARITY**  
   A defensible recovered method reproduces the result.

2. **SUBSTANTIVE DIVERGENCE**  
   The authors' method is reconstructed with reasonable confidence but the number still disagrees.

3. **NON-IDENTIFIABLE**  
   Multiple materially different methods reproduce the target and cannot be distinguished.

4. **UNRECOVERABLE**  
   Required inputs or methodological information cannot be obtained.

5. **OUT OF SCOPE / INFEASIBLE**  
   Recovery exceeds project constraints.

These outcomes are more useful than a generic “cannot replicate”.

---

## Top-level metrics

The framework should report separately:

### Blind Parity Rate

How much of the paper can be independently reproduced from its stated methodology and blind evidence boundary.

This partly measures **paper reproducibility / methodological completeness**.

### Recovered Parity Rate

How much can ultimately be reconstructed after method recovery using the wider available evidence base.

This more directly measures **replication-system capability**.

### Unresolved Rate

Claims that remain:

- non-identifiable;
- unrecoverable;
- or substantively divergent.

Only this residual set should support a strong statement such as:

> “We could not reproduce this result.”

---

# 3. Why ASASSN-21qj motivates this stage

Several apparent failures were later shown to be recoverable or strongly suggestive of hidden method choices:

- transverse velocity: fine segmentation plus a diameter convention recovers approximately `7.9 km/s` versus `7.5 km/s`;
- eclipse timing: a roughly 20% threshold reproduces reported start, end, and duration together;
- ATLAS counts: intra-night pair treatment comes close to published counts while literal nightly averaging does not;
- wavelength-depth ordering: correcting the forced survey zero-point mismatch restores strict monotonic ordering;
- pre-brightening photosphere disagreement is largely explained by the simplified stellar model and incomplete propagated uncertainty.

Under blind rules these appropriately remain failures or diagnostics.

Under a production replication objective they should enter a formal parity-recovery stage rather than terminate as “cannot replicate”.

# 4. Final Red-Team Iteration Must Be Bounded

The current run has shown that the red-team layer is valuable.

It has caught real scientific and implementation errors.

However, **16 red-team iterations and roughly eight hours at the final gate is too much**.

That is not a desirable steady-state feature of the harness.

It means the review process is currently doing too much work that should either:

1. happen earlier;
2. be automated mechanically; or
3. be consolidated into fewer review/remediation cycles.

The goal is to preserve the quality of the red team while making convergence an explicit property of the harness.

---

## The final review should operate on a frozen candidate

FINAL should not be a period of continuous building plus continuous reviewing.

The intended structure should be:

**Freeze candidate → review → batch remediation → closure review**

A reviewer should assess a stable candidate rather than repeatedly chasing a moving repository.

---

## Run the three perspectives in parallel

For a frozen FINAL candidate:

- red team;
- domain expert;
- practitioner;

should run independently and in parallel.

They should not see each other's verdicts.

Their findings are then consolidated into **one remediation batch**.

This avoids:

> red team → fix → red team → fix → domain → fix → red team again → practitioner → fix → red team again

which creates unnecessary serial depth.

---

## Require reviewers to bundle findings

A reviewer should return its full set of material findings in one pass.

It should not drip-feed:

> blocker 1 → fix → blocker 2 → fix → blocker 3

when blockers 2 and 3 were already discoverable in the original candidate.

Each review should explicitly classify findings by severity.

Suggested:

- `P0` — invalidates the deliverable or a load-bearing scientific claim;
- `P1` — material defect that must be fixed before the gate;
- `P2` — real but non-blocking limitation/correction;
- `P3` — style, cleanup, or future improvement.

Only P0/P1 findings reopen the gate.

---

## Suggested review-cycle limit

For each gate:

### Pass 1 — Full review

Fresh-context, broad adversarial review of the frozen candidate.

Output: one complete blocker set.

### Remediation 1 — Batch fix

Fix all accepted P0/P1 findings together.

Run mechanical regression checks.

### Pass 2 — Targeted closure review

Reviewer checks:

- previous blockers;
- changed areas;
- regressions caused by fixes;
- any immediately adjacent load-bearing claims.

This is **not another unrestricted full review**.

### Remediation 2 — Final batch if needed

Only remaining P0/P1 issues.

### Pass 3 — Closure

Final targeted confirmation.

**Default hard cap: 3 review passes per perspective per gate.**

A fourth pass should require either:

- a newly introduced P0 defect caused by remediation; or
- explicit human approval.

Beyond that, the gate stops as `UNRESOLVED` rather than allowing indefinite autonomous iteration.

---

## New findings after Pass 1

Later passes should not freely broaden scope.

A genuinely new P0/P1 defect can still block.

But a new P2/P3 issue found on pass 2 or 3 should be logged and carried forward rather than reopening the project.

Otherwise adversarial review has no natural stopping condition: a capable model can always find another sentence to improve.

---

## Review the delta, not the whole project

After the first full review, subsequent passes should receive:

- the previous findings;
- remediation record;
- changed files/artefacts;
- relevant regenerated outputs;
- regression results.

They should inspect the affected causal neighbourhood, not start from zero over the entire repository.

A full re-review is justified only when a fix changes:

- core methodology;
- data selection;
- a major dependency;
- acceptance logic;
- a large part of the final report.

---

## Convert repeated red-team catches into mechanical gates

This is one of the most important lessons from ASASSN-21qj.

If the red team catches the **same class of error twice**, the framework should ask whether it can become an automated check.

Examples already earned by the run include:

- prose claiming a generator exists when it does not;
- declared artefacts missing;
- stage-map reads/outputs inconsistent with implementation;
- hard-coded scientific verdicts;
- stale generated reports;
- silent no-op edits;
- self-referential expectations;
- quarantined numerical values leaking into active state;
- perturbations failing to reach final verdicts.

Once mechanical, these should stop consuming red-team cycles.

The long-run goal is:

> reviewers spend their time on scientific reasoning and genuinely novel failure modes, not repository hygiene already discovered on previous papers.

---

## Final-gate stopping rule

FINAL should close when:

1. all P0 findings are closed;
2. all accepted P1 findings are closed or explicitly human-waived;
3. mechanical battery is clean;
4. three reviewer perspectives have given a closure verdict;
5. remaining issues are P2/P3 or explicitly stated limitations.

It should **not** require the absence of any possible criticism.

That standard is unattainable and produces endless reviewer recursion.

---

## What 16 iterations means

The current 16-iteration final red-team sequence should be treated as a **calibration result**, not the desired operating model.

It was valuable because it exposed recurring generic weaknesses in an early version of the harness.

But once those failure classes have been converted into framework rules and tests, repeating sixteen passes on future papers would indicate that the review architecture has not improved.

A mature run should probably look more like:

> **1 full review + 1 remediation batch + 1 closure review**

with an occasional third pass on genuinely difficult projects.

Not sixteen.

---

# 5. Overall design principle

The framework should be strict in two different ways:

### On science

Do not manufacture parity.

Freeze the blind result, recover hidden methodology explicitly, and preserve the difference.

### On review

Do not mistake infinite criticism for rigour.

Review must be adversarial **and convergent**.

The strongest harness is not the one whose red team can keep finding something forever.

It is the one that:

- catches material defects;
- turns recurring defect classes into mechanical protection;
- fixes them in batches;
- and knows when the remaining limitations are honest enough to stop.
